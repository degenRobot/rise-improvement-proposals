Synthetic book

A Papertrade-style perps book where every position is backed by another trader or by a real order on RISEx

@degenRobot · mattsmart.xyz · November 2026 · An experiment on RISE testnet, live since 13 November 2026

Traders get the simplicity of a synthetic perps app: one pool, one price, no order book to read. Behind it, traders are matched with each other at the RISEx mid, and whoever is left waits behind a single resting order on RISEx. The pool never takes a side, so liquidity providers hold no directional exposure. The book can offer more leverage than RISEx does, and the risk of it stays in the book's pool, not in RISEx's risk engine.

No LP directional riskThe book's net position always equals its RISEx position. Measured on a fork: a 2% move changed pool value by under 0.05 USDC.
Leverage risk stays in the bookOn a fork, traders ran at 94× while RISEx margined the book's one account at 4×. A 3% gap cost the pool 8% and left RISEx's liquidator nothing to do.
Real liquidity, not a quoteThe unmatched side rests on RISEx as one post-only order sized to the queue, so venue liquidity fills it and RISEx gets the book's net flow.
One atomic transactionSettle fills, match, queue and re-place the RISEx order together. That is why it needs the order role (RIP‑4).

How a trade flows

Where the leverage risk sits

The book's leverage limit is its own setting. RISEx never sees a trader's position, only the book's account, which holds the whole pool and every trader's margin against the book's net position. So a trader can be at 100× while the account RISEx margins is at a few times.

Then the price gapped down 3% before any liquidation ran. Every trader was bankrupt, having lost about 310 USD against 110 of margin. This is where the loss went:

In the orderly case the pool pays nothing. One trader at 94× with 22 USDC of margin was liquidated after an 80 bp move: the trader kept 2.97 USDC, the keeper earned 2.05, and pool value did not change.

What this means for
RISExA resting post-only order inside the spread, taker flow from market closes and liquidations, and the book's net position as open interest. Trades matched inside the book never reach RISEx: it gets net flow, not gross.
The book's LPsThe pool is the insurance fund. At 100×, any gap over 1% before a liquidation is the pool's loss.
The book's settingsThe mid-to-mark gate must fit between initial and maintenance margin, or the contract refuses the settings: under 50 bp at 100× with 0.5% maintenance. Testnet ETH runs 1% at 20×. Testnet BTC, whose mid sits about 1% from the mark, could not run 100×.

How it compares with Papertrade

Papertrade on Hyperliquid made synthetic perps popular. Every trade there is a swap between the user and the LP, priced at Hyperliquid's mid, and the docs say no perpetual contracts are traded on Hyperliquid. The LP is every trader's counterparty and carries their net, unhedged.

What the queue book gives up: a trader may wait for a fill, a liquidation can walk RISEx's book, and the design needs RISEx's order role.

What the resting order becomes

Step 5 leaves one order on RISEx. Its side and size depend on who was waiting when the trade arrived:

Queue before a long arrivesWhat happensResting order after
EmptyThe long joins the queueBuy, the long's size
Shorts, larger than the longThe long matches the oldest shorts at the midSell, reduced by the long's size
Shorts, smaller than the longAll shorts match; the rest starts a long queueBuy, the leftover
LongsThe long joins the backBuy, the new total, at the new mid

Liquidation

Anyone may call either route once a trader is below maintenance.

  • liquidate(trader): waiting traders on the other side take what they can at mid; the rest is sold on RISEx in the same transaction. Fast, but a large position walks the book.
  • liquidateToQueue(trader): waiting traders take what they can; the rest goes to the front of the queue and leaves through the resting order or the next opposite trader, so nothing walks the book. liquidate can finish it at any time.

Why it needs RIP‑4

The resting order on RISEx must always equal the queue. If the book settled, matched and queued in one transaction but sent the order through the hosted API in another, the two would drift apart between transactions, and the pool would carry the gap. Only a contract holding RISEx's order role can change both together. The book also routes orders for books in other markets: it presents a router permit whose signer is the other book, and that book's isValidSignature approves only the permit being presented for it at that moment.

The five steps of a trade, simplified from the testnet contract:

function _order(address trader, int256 delta) internal {
    _sync();                           // 1. settle what RISEx filled, read from the book's position
    _cancelResting();                  //    and take the old order off RISEx
    _gate(mid, mark, maxMidMarkBps);   // 2. size caps, and the mid inside a gate of the mark
    uint256 left = _matchQueue(trader, delta, mid);   // 3. match waiting traders at the mid
    if (left != 0) _enqueue(trader, left);            // 4. the remainder joins the queue
    _requireMargin(trader, mark);
    _placeResting();                   // 5. one post-only order on RISEx, sized to the queue
}

_cancelResting and _placeResting call RISEx directly; they are the two lines that need the order role.

Venue facts the build turned up

  • RISEx deletes filled and cancelled orders and reuses their IDs, so the book reads fills from its position, never from a remembered order ID.
  • A post-only order that would cross reverts, so a resting buy rounds the mid down and a sell rounds it up.
  • Orders expire on fixed 5-minute clock boundaries, and an expired order stays in the open-order list until something touches it.
