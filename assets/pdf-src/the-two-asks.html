RISE Improvement Proposals · Summary

Two asks of RISEx

Let contract accounts sign for themselves, and publish how a contract earns the order role

@degenRobot · mattsmart.xyz · November 2026 · Drafts RIP‑3 and RIP‑4

A contract account can hold collateral on RISEx and validate signatures on chain. It cannot trade under its own rules through the hosted API, and it cannot trade in the same transaction as its own logic. Each fix is small for RISE: one is in the API, the other is a process.

RIP‑3 · Contract signatures on the hosted API

The ask
Accept a permit whose signer is a contract and whose signature is longer than 65 bytes. Pre-check the account's isValidSignature, then relay through the router's existing ERC-1271 path.
What changes for RISE
The API only. The router already decodes these permits.
What it opens
Passkey wallets, RISE Wallet included. Safe treasuries. Agent wallets with limits. Lending where an order that breaks the loan is an invalid signature. Stop-losses anyone can relay.
Main risk, and its bound
The API runs code that accounts wrote. A gas cap on the check, per-address quotas, and a throttle on accounts whose permits fail on chain.
Evidence
On 13 November 2026 the API refused a 388-byte signature that the account itself accepted. Of 2,715 recently active accounts sampled, none is a contract.

RIP‑4 · A process for the order role

The ask
Publish how a contract applies for AccessManager role 14, the tiers from testnet to mainnet, and when the role is revoked.
What changes for RISE
No code. A registry page, and grants made with zero execution delay.
What it opens
The synthetic book. Order hooks. Liquidating a credit or portfolio-margin account in one transaction, which RISE's own RIP‑2 describes.
Main risk, and its bound
A holder skips the API's limits and may pool client funds in one account. Notional and rate caps, an audit, timelocked upgrades, and revocation that takes effect at once.
Evidence
RISE granted the role to three experimental contracts on testnet on 13 November 2026. Every path that had been waiting ran live the same day.

What RISE gets

Accounts that cannot trade todayMultisig treasuries, passkey wallets and lending protocols have no path through the API until RIP‑3.
Flow it does not have to buildThe synthetic book rests a post-only order at the mid and sends closes and liquidations to RISEx's book, under a builder code. RISEx gets the book's net flow, not its gross.
Risk that stays outside its engineOn a fork, traders at 94× showed to RISEx as one account at 3.95×. A 3% gap cost the book's pool 8% and left RISEx's liquidator nothing to do.
